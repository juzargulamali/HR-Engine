# 8. Decisions Log

Six judgment calls were flagged in the design review (§ Decisions needed) as reasonable defaults
that were genuinely the business's to confirm. All six were reviewed and decided on 2026-09-22.
Recorded here so the rationale survives past the conversation that produced it — the same
traceability principle applied to every approval and balance change in the product itself.

| # | Question | Decision | Rationale | Design impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Can employees see their own bank account/IBAN? | **Yes, read-only.** HR Admin/Finance remain the only roles that can write it. | Lets employees self-verify the details on file before payday without any write risk. | Matches the schema/RLS as already designed (`docs/02-database-schema.md` §2.3, `compensation_select` policy) — no change required. |
| 2 | Does the CEO authorize every payroll-variable export, or only above a threshold? | **Every export, unconditionally.** | Enginious wants executive sign-off on every payroll run regardless of size, not just exceptions. | **Changed the design.** `payroll_export_run` is now the one approval workflow with no conditional steps — Finance (step 1) then CEO (step 2) are both mandatory every time. Updated `docs/03-permission-matrix.md` §3.6, `docs/04-user-journeys.md` §4.8–4.9, `docs/05-automation-rules.md` §5.3. |
| 3 | Does Polish/EU employee data need to stay in a separate region from UAE/KSA data? | **No special requirement.** | No client/contract currently demands in-region EU storage; a single Supabase project keeps operations simple for a small team. | Matches the architecture as already designed (`docs/01-architecture.md` §1.4, single project/database). Revisit if a future contract requires EU data residency — see `docs/07-risk-register.md` risk 10. |
| 4 | Is "anonymize personal details, keep payroll/audit numbers" acceptable for GDPR erasure requests? | **Yes.** | Compliant under GDPR's legal-obligation exception for payroll/labor records; avoids destroying the numeric ledger/audit history the rest of the design depends on. | Matches the mitigation already documented for `docs/07-risk-register.md` risk 9 — no change required. Still a manual, jointly-authorized procedure (HR Admin + Sys Admin), never a self-service delete. |
| 5 | Which country pilots first? | **UAE** — confirmed as headquarters, with Saudi Arabia and Poland as smaller satellite offices today. | UAE has the fullest role coverage and headcount, making it the strongest validation of the whole system before extending to the other two countries. | Updated `docs/06-implementation-phases.md` (new "Company context" section, Phase 7 rollout note) to reflect UAE as the primary build target rather than an equal third of initial scope. |
| 6 | Direct-to-main or PR-reviewed workflow once implementation starts? | **Direct to main for now.** | Matches how the design package itself was delivered; fastest iteration while it's a small team. | No design change. Revisit once more than one person is committing code — a PR-based workflow can be adopted at any point without any structural rework. |

## What this means going into Phase 0

- The only structural change from this round is decision 2 (mandatory two-step payroll
  authorization) — already reflected in the documents above.
- Decisions 1, 3, 4, and 6 confirm defaults that required no design change.
- Decision 5 sets delivery sequencing, not architecture: the schema and policy engine remain
  identical for all three countries; UAE simply goes first end-to-end.

## Account Control & Security Settings (2026-09-25)

One further judgment call, raised during the security review of that feature's implementation
(commit `f6870ccfe4469557a1f0bbdaaeb23a663b8194b9`), needed the same explicit sign-off rather than
inheriting an assumption:

| # | Question | Decision | Rationale | Design impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | Should System Administrator's authority to activate/deactivate an account, resend an invitation, or trigger a password reset be scoped per company, or remain global? | **Global, approved as-is.** System Admin may manage accounts across all companies. HR Admin remains unable to activate/deactivate accounts, invite users, resend invitations, or trigger password resets — that authority stays Sys-Admin-only, unchanged by this decision. | Matches the pre-existing, already-shipped scope of every other Sys-Admin-gated action (`inviteUser`, `deleteUserAccount`, `assignRole` in `lib/actions/users.ts`) and `docs/03-permission-matrix.md` §3.6 ("Manage roles / user access" — Sys Admin: F, no company qualifier). `has_role('sys_admin')` called with no company argument (as `set_account_status()` and `log_security_event()` in `supabase/migrations/20261104000000_account_security_controls.sql` both do) structurally requires an *unscoped* grant — a company-scoped `sys_admin` row satisfies nothing anywhere in this codebase, so in practice every real Sys Admin grant already is global. | **No design change** — this feature inherits the existing scope model rather than introducing a new one. **Revisit before HR Engine becomes a true multi-company/SaaS product**: at that point, a single global Sys Admin role able to deactivate any tenant's users is very likely the wrong model, and this decision (along with `docs/03-permission-matrix.md` §3.6 generally) should be re-opened rather than carried forward by default. |

## Recovery Leave: working periods and 24-elapsed-hour windows (2026-10)

Design decisions taken while implementing the "final attendance and recovery redesign"
(see [recovery-windows-deployment.md](./recovery-windows-deployment.md)):

| # | Question | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | Replace the old calculation for everyone at once? | **No: additive and dormant.** Sessions are stamped `legacy` or `windowed` at clock-in. Only a policy with `model = recovery_windows`, activated through a controlled function with a future effective date, makes new clock-ins `windowed`. | Nothing in history is recalculated; sessions in flight finish under the rules they started with; the migration can be applied and verified before any behaviour changes. |
| 9 | Where does the calculation run? | **In the database**, in one engine (`recovery_derive` / `recovery_recalculate_employee`) reached by triggers on the raw evidence tables and by the processor; a TypeScript twin (`packages/domain/src/recoveryWindows.ts`) pins the same rules with shared boundary scenarios. | Every lifecycle path (clock in/out, mode switch, HR close/correction/add-missing, processor) must share one authoritative lifecycle; readiness must be enforceable inside `decide_leave_approval()`, not only in the UI. |
| 10 | How are post-approval changes credited? | **Only the difference**, as a new request tied to the same window (`window_top_up` / `window_reduction`); reductions reverse and re-earn with the original dates; reductions that touch used credit need an explicit HR acknowledgement. | Compatible with the one-active-earned-credit-per-reference ledger guard, never extends the window's expiry, never a silent negative balance. |
| 11 | Alerts when the work stopped before a boundary? | A rollover is raised only for a 24h boundary the work actually **carries across** (an open or continuing session), never for a boundary that falls after the last recorded work. | Avoids noise for someone who clocked out hours before the boundary and rested. |
| 12 | Scheduler | **pg_cron every 5 minutes (owner-enabled) as primary; a daily Vercel route as a safety net.** Vercel Hobby cannot schedule more often than daily, so it is never described as live until a successful run is observed. | Honest about the platform limit; the processor is idempotent so overlap and delay are safe. |
| 13 | Manual daily totals under the new policy | They never create a credit by themselves; they are flagged for review. HR records exact times with "Add missing attendance". | A typed total cannot prove gaps, rest or window boundaries. |
| 14 | What happens to a working period when a policy is activated or switched off in the middle of it? | **The period keeps the rules it started under.** A restart less than the 8 h rest threshold after an earlier session inherits that session's model (legacy stays legacy across an activation; windowed stays windowed across a switch-off); only a rest of 8 h or more lets the policy then in force decide. Each period also snapshots its policy version and rules. | A period split across two calculations could be judged twice or not at all; this makes "one period = one policy" a database rule, covered by tests. Replaces the earlier "sessions after a deactivation date are legacy" limitation. |
| 15 | Who may activate / switch off a windows policy? | **The same people as every other policy version**: an HR Admin or the CEO/CTO under `has_role(..., null, country)` (a country-limited grant is enough — nothing global), never the drafter; the CEO/CTO activate only on the date HR already set on the draft. Switch-off is a **new** capability for the same people, future-dated, touching no evidence. | My first design wrongly narrowed activation to unscoped HR Admins and excluded the CEO/CTO. The only existing-code change is one guard-function exception (`guard_policy_version_update`) for the controlled function, so the date and the previous version's end date can be set without giving the CEO/CTO edit rights. |
| 16 | Is the 5-minute scheduler optional? | **No: it is a verified prerequisite for activation.** `activate_recovery_windows_policy()` refuses unless pg_cron has the active job and a successful run started by it within 15 minutes. Switching a policy off never stops the processor; it may only be retired (guarded block) once no windowed period, open session, failure or unrouted request remains. | Without it, windows close and alerts appear once a day; and running periods still need processing after a switch-off. |
| 17 | How do we know the app and the SQL we run are the same database? | A one-row **database fingerprint** stamped by the migration, shown (8 characters) on the HR Alerts page and printed by the verification SQL. | The old Supabase project was deleted; this proves the deployed app is bound to Enginious HR Engine_V2 without displaying any URL, ref or key. |
| 18 | Legacy attendance specs that describe the previous calculation | They read the rules marker on the manual tool and **skip, with the reason, once the windows policy is in force**; the window-based end-to-end credit spec replaces them. | Known-incompatible tests are updated in this release rather than left to fail after activation. |
| 19 | Where do a CEO's or CTO's own requests go? | **They need no approver.** Leave is approved on submission (through the normal approval finalisation, so balances and ledgers behave as for any approved leave); reimbursement claims go straight to **Finance**; a Recovery Leave credit is **approved automatically when nothing blocks it**, and anything that needs a human (HR verification of unusual cases, a correction made after approval) goes to the HR queue. A person who holds both CEO/CTO and HR Admin is treated as an executive. Timesheets, letters and payroll runs are unchanged. The employee profile no longer shows "No manager assigned" for a CEO/CTO. | Nothing sits above the top of the org chart; the previous fallback ("any *other* active CEO/CTO") broke as soon as only one executive existed, and made two executives approve each other. Auto-approval reuses `decide_leave_approval()`, so the finalisation logic exists once. The blockers (closed and verified window, current amount) are the same ones that stop any other credit. **Deployment:** migration `20261109000000_executive_requests_no_approver.sql` (additive: two helpers and `create or replace` of `create_initial_approval`, `recovery_route_for_employee`, `recovery_route_request`; no table or data change). App: `resolveInitialApprover` mirrors it. Known leftover: the "leave submitted" email still says "Review it" for an auto-approved executive leave. |
| 20 | Should the two balance views keep running with their owner's rights? | **No: `security_invoker = true`.** `leave_balances` and `comp_day_balances` were plain views owned by `postgres`, so they bypassed the ledgers' row-level security and anyone holding SELECT (including not-signed-in `anon`) could read every employee's balance in every company. The views now run as the caller, `anon` has no access, signed-in users keep SELECT only, and two additive ledger policies keep CEO/CTO (leave) and Finance + CEO/CTO (Recovery Leave) able to read the balances they could see before. Balances and calculations are unchanged (view definitions untouched). | Applied manually after read-only confirmation of the real V2 definitions (`supabase/manual-sql/balance_views_00_confirm_readonly.sql`) and verified with `balance_views_20_verify_readonly.sql`. Migration `20261112000000`. |
